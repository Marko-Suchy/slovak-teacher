# Notes for “V klientskom centre”

This is a fictional A1+ language exercise, not a document checklist or a determination of anyone's citizenship. It assumes Marek's father **was a Slovak citizen when Marek was born**. The current Slovak citizenship law's birth rule refers to a parent who is a citizen; the exact rules applicable to a real person can depend on their birth date and other facts. [Slovak citizenship law, § 5](https://www.slov-lex.sk/ezbierky/pravne-predpisy/SK/ZZ/1993/40)

The official terms practiced in the story are **rodný list** (birth certificate) and **osvedčenie o štátnom občianstve Slovenskej republiky** (certificate of Slovak citizenship). The certificate is issued to prove citizenship after an application; it is not one of the papers Marek brings to request it. The official public-service portal says an application is submitted in person and lists an identity document, a birth certificate, and, where relevant, other documents. A Ministry of Interior guidance PDF also lists parents' birth certificates and says an office may request additional documents. The exact documents needed for a real case should be confirmed with the office. [Public-service portal](https://www.slovensko.sk/sk/zivotne-situacie/zivotna-situacia/_potrebujem-osvedcenie-o-statno), [Ministry of Interior guidance](https://www.minv.sk/swift_data/source/miestna_statna_sprava/ou_zilina1/ovvs/oznamy_a_informacie/2023/k_ziadosti_o_vydanie_osvedcenia_a_potvrdenie_o_so_sr_je_potrebne_predlozit.pdf)

The story deliberately does not promise that Marek receives the certificate during the visit. The public-service portal says it is generally issued within 30 days after application. [Public-service portal](https://www.slovensko.sk/sk/zivotne-situacie/zivotna-situacia/_potrebujem-osvedcenie-o-statno)
